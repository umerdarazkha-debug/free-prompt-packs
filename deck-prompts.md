# The 5 prompts: turn a fact sheet into an argument

"Make me a presentation" gives you clean slides, but they're usually just a list of facts.
Send these 5 prompts **one at a time** in the same chat, and you get one argument with speaker notes.

---

## 0. Give it your facts first
Paste your notes, numbers and sources, then add:

> Only use these facts. Don't invent numbers. If something is missing, mark it [ADD].

## 1. Structure first
> Give me the storyline as 8 slide headlines that read like one argument. No slides yet.

Read the 8 headlines out loud. If they don't build to one point, ask for another storyline before going on.

## 2. One idea per slide
> Now build the slides. Each slide makes one point. The headline is the takeaway, not a topic label.

## 3. Design notes
> Max 2 fonts, 1 accent colour, no bullet walls, one visual per slide. For each slide, say which visual you chose and why, then apply it.

## 4. Speaker notes
> Write what I say for each slide, in 3 short spoken lines, so the slide doesn't have to say it.

## 5. Critic pass
> Act as a tough audience. Name the 3 weakest slides and fix them.

---

**Before you present:** check every number against your source. The critic pass catches a lot, but not everything.

Tested on a real topic (Tim Hortons in Pakistan, sourced facts): the plain prompt gave 10 clean fact slides in about a minute; these 5 prompts gave 8 slides that make one argument, plus speaker notes, in about 4 minutes.
